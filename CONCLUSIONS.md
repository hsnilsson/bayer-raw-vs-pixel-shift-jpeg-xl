# Conclusions

The study asks whether a high-resolution PS16 scan compressed to a conventional RAW61 byte budget retains useful information. The corrected [report](site/index.html) answers the measured parts of that question with explicit capture cohorts, exact byte counts and separate color and structure comparisons.

Rendered JPEG XL fidelity is measured against the retained PS16 render. The relative RAW61 baseline includes differences in capture, noise, demosaic, color interpretation, scaling and registration. A candidate closer to PS16 is not thereby proved more faithful to the physical film. Native-crop results are essential: small errors after a tenfold linear-light reduction cannot establish preservation of native grain or detail.

The d=2.0 strong-loss controls also meet the relative color and structure screens, despite visible changes in inspected crops. This demonstrates that those screens cannot define acceptable archival quality. The report shows their continuous errors and keeps these controls outside the decision grid.

The equally prominent DNG route reaches its separate 200 MiB target for all sixteen historical d=0.01 files and passes the technical audit. Lossless files pass the technical audit but none meets 200 MiB. Exact pixel claims in this repair apply to the checked crops. A common validated render and inversion comparison is still needed to establish any DNG quality advantage over RAW61.

The previous camera-RGB DeltaE, cross-domain error ratios and 15/16 archive-value qualification are withdrawn. The combiner comparison measures tonal agreement with a noisy source ARW, not recovered latitude. Controlled exposure brackets address capture latitude separately.

These are descriptive results from a limited corpus, not a universal archival recommendation or approval to discard source masters. Owner visual review and publication remain separate actions. See the [release evidence](site/data/release.json) and [limitations](LIMITATIONS.md).
